# Platform Update Snapshot — 2026-09-13

## Google Search
Google Search Central's June 2026 update log says Google clarified that llms.txt files are not needed for Google Search and do not positively or negatively affect visibility or rankings. It also records a May 2026 guide for optimizing generative AI features, emphasizing non-commodity content, local/shopping/image/video content, AI-agent guidance and continued relevance of SEO fundamentals. FAQ rich results were deprecated for Search in May 2026.

OPE implication: llms.txt is optional and must never be treated as a Google ranking requirement. AEO/GEO work remains grounded in strong search fundamentals and useful, non-commodity content.

## Bing
Bing introduced AI Performance in Webmaster Tools public preview in February 2026. It exposes total citations, average cited pages, grounding queries, page-level citation activity and visibility trends across supported AI experiences. Bing explicitly says these signals do not indicate ranking, authority or page importance.

OPE implication: AI citation/grounding measurement is now a real instrumentation layer and should be tracked separately from classic rankings.

Bing also describes grounding as a layer connecting AI systems with current authoritative information and emphasizes structured, verifiable and applicable content.

## OpenAI / ChatGPT Search
OpenAI's current publisher guidance says public websites can appear in ChatGPT search and recommends allowing OAI-SearchBot when publishers want content discoverable, surfaced and cited. OpenAI also notes that host/CDN configuration must permit the published searchbot traffic. ChatGPT referrals can be measured through analytics; OpenAI documents a utm_source value for these referrals.

## Governance
These observations are platform-specific adapters, not universal ranking laws. Re-check official documentation before every release and record changes in the source registry.
